# Getting your first client

Where to look, what to say first, what car buyers ask and how to answer, and
how to offer the setup. The product and the prices are in
[business.md](business.md).

The messages and answers below are starting points: change the words until
they sound like you, and keep what works.

## Before you write to anyone

1. **Know the product from both sides.** Go through the
   [live demo](https://ilyaxxing.github.io/cash-for-cars-lead-system/#demo)
   as a seller, then tap the notification and work the lead as the buyer.
2. **Pick who to approach.** The best first client already hands out cards or
   puts up signs and answers the phone themselves: they already pay to find
   sellers, and they see the point in a minute. A buyer who gets every car
   from ads or bought leads wants more sellers, and this system does not
   bring sellers. It catches the ones who already saw a card, a sign or a
   listing.
3. **Make their demo.** In the link maker on the
   [project page](https://ilyaxxing.github.io/cash-for-cars-lead-system/#show),
   type the business's name and pick its color. The link opens the page for
   car buyers in their own name, with the live demo and no prices or setup
   details. Send that link, not this repository.

## Where to find them

- **Cards under wipers and road signs.** The phone number is on them. Keep
  the card or take a photo: "I found your card" is an honest way to start.
- **Google Maps.** Search "cash for cars", "we buy cars" and "junk car buyer"
  with your city. A listing with no website, or with a site that shows only
  a phone number, has the most to gain.
- **Facebook Marketplace, local groups and Craigslist.** Accounts that post
  "we buy cars, any condition".
- **Tow companies, repair shops and junkyards.** They know every buyer in
  town, and an introduction works better than a cold message.

Start where you live: a buyer you can meet is easier to win than one in
another state. List twenty businesses before you write to the first, and
expect most of them not to answer.

## The first message

Write to one business at a time, by hand, as yourself: what you saw, what you
do, and the demo link. Fill in the brackets, and drop any line that is not
true for you.

**A text to the number on a card or a sign**

> Hi, is this [business name]? I found your card on my windshield on
> [street]. I add a QR code to car buyers' cards: the seller scans it and
> sends the car with photos, and it reaches your phone in seconds. I made a
> demo in your name: [link]. Want to see how it would work for you?

**A message on Facebook, or to a Maps listing**

> Hi! I saw your "we buy cars" post in [group]. I set up a simple system for
> car buyers: a link and QR codes for your posts, cards and signs. The seller
> sends the car and photos, and you get a notification on your phone right
> away. Here is a demo with your name on it: [link]. Can I show you in five
> minutes?

**An email**

> Subject: A demo I made for [business name]
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I found [business name] on Google Maps. I set up lead systems for car
> buyers: a QR code and a link for your cards, signs and listings, a short
> form the seller fills in on their phone, and a notification on yours the
> moment they leave their number.
>
> I made a demo in your name, so you can try both sides: [link]
>
> If it looks useful, I can show you the rest in five minutes. If you would
> rather not hear from me again, just say so.
>
> [your name]
> [your city and mailing address]
> [your phone]

**A call, or in person**

Twenty seconds, then the demo on your phone:

> Hi, I'm [name], I live here in [city]. You hand out cards, right? I add a
> QR code to them: the seller scans it and sends you the car with photos,
> and your phone buzzes right away. Can I show you? It takes a minute.

Hand them your phone with their demo open, and let them fill in the form
themselves.

**No answer?** One reminder after two or three days, then leave them be:

> Hi, [name] again. The demo for [business name] is still here: [link]. If
> it is not for you, no problem.

**Rules for cold messages.** One by one, by hand, to a number or an address
the business itself advertises. No bulk or automated texts and calls: in the
US those need consent given beforehand (the TCPA), and a commercial email has
to say who sent it, carry a postal address and offer a way to opt out
(CAN-SPAM). Stop when someone says no. Other countries have their own rules.
This is not legal advice.

## The price in numbers

Promise nothing. Say what it costs and let them do the arithmetic.

- **One car pays for it.** If a car earns them a few hundred dollars, one
  extra car covers the setup.
- **Compared with bought leads.** Lead vendors list junk car leads at
  roughly $20-30 each (their public price pages, November 2026; check what
  vendors charge in the client's area). At $25 a lead, a $400 setup is the
  price of 16 bought leads, paid once. A lead from their own card reaches
  them alone, while a vendor may sell the same seller to other buyers too.
- **What they already spend.** A thousand cards usually cost under $150 to
  print, plus the time to hand them out. Today only the people willing to
  call answer. The code adds those who are not, on the same cards.
- **What it cannot do.** It does not bring sellers by itself. Leads come
  from the cards, signs and links the business puts out; with none of those,
  nothing arrives. Say so before they pay.

## What they ask, and what to answer

**"My sellers just call me."** The phone number stays on the card. The code
is for people who will not call a stranger: at work, late at night, or not
ready to talk yet. You lose no callers and gain the others.

**"Nobody will fill in a form."** Try the demo: three short steps about the
car, then the phone number, and the lead is already on your phone. Everything
after that is optional: history, price, photos. How many sellers go on, the
first weeks will show: the statistics count the sellers from every place.

**"I already have a website."** Or a Facebook page. Then you already have
places for the link. The site keeps doing its job; the link goes into the
Maps listing, the profile and the posts, each counted separately, so you see
which one brings cars. And a lead reaches your phone in seconds, not an inbox
you read at night.

**"How much?"** The setup price, once, and what it includes. Then the
arithmetic above. Hosting, the domain and printing are paid by the business
directly: say so in the same breath, with the amounts.

**"What is Railway, and why do I need a domain?"** Railway is the hosting
company the system runs on, from $5 a month on your own card. The address,
such as `buy.yourbusiness.com`, is printed inside every QR code, so it has to
be yours for good. Both are in your name so that the system and the leads
stay yours even if I disappear. I do the technical part; you sign up, add a
card and accept the hand-over.

**"Do I need to install an app?"** Nothing from an app store. The dashboard
is added to the phone's home screen from the browser, and we do it together
in ten minutes. An iPhone needs iOS 16.4 or later. The same login works on a
second phone, so a partner gets the notifications too.

**"Who sees my sellers?"** Only you. The leads are in your own database, on
your own account. Sellers' names, phones and photos are erased 90 days after
a lead is closed.

**"Is leaving cards on cars even allowed here?"** It depends on the city and
on who owns the parking lot: some ban it, and some fine for it. Check the
local rules. The code works the same on a sticker on your own car, a sign, a
poster at a partner shop and the link in your Maps listing.

**"What if it brings nothing?"** I cannot promise sellers: that depends on
how many cards, signs and links are out there. What I can show is that it
works: the second half is due only after a test lead from your own card
reaches your phone.

## How to offer it

**Half now, half when it works.** Half of the setup price before you start,
the rest when a test lead from their own card arrives on their phone. They
see it working before the second payment.

Put it in writing, one message is enough: what is included, the price, when
it is due, and what monthly support covers if they take it (the table in
[business.md](business.md#pricing)).

## When they say yes

Ask for what the setup needs; Claude asks for it step by step, following
[SETUP.md](../SETUP.md):

- the business's name and phone, and who runs it (for the privacy policy);
- a domain, or a subdomain of their website;
- a Railway account on their own card;
- their logo and color, if they have them;
- ten minutes with the owner's phone.

Then open Claude Code and send the line from the
[README](../README.md#get-started).

A month in, open the statistics together: sellers and cars bought, from each
place. With their permission, those numbers are your case for the next
client.

Claude Code can help with the selling too. Open this repository in it and
ask, for example: *"Write my first text to Rapid Cash Cars. I found their
card on Main Street."* In another country, have it write in the buyers'
language, and check the local rules for unsolicited messages first.
